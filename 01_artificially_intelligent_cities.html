ARTIFICIALLY INTELLIGENT CITIES: ALGORITHMIC CONTROL NEGOTIATED BY HUMAN UNREASON

Every day the built environment becomes more monitored, more automated, more responsive, and more controlled. Institutional “smart” cities weaponize urban technology. Sensing becomes hidden surveillance, reasoning becomes algorithmic prediction, and acting becomes spatial control. The resulting condition contradicts the city as a stage for liberty, equality, and fraternity—values now replaced by the technocratic ideals of comfort, security, and sustainability. It eliminates the unpredictability of complex human nature that defines public life and constructs a frictionless and automated experience.

The dissertation investigates the power of urban networks and demands an ideological reversal. It accepts the inevitable intrusion of technology and uses it as a support for spatial negotiation guided by three core principles: participation, transparency, and friction. The resulting framework presents an alternative for future cities. It preserves the citizen as the protagonist of the urban narrative, resisting a reduction to a data source. The goal is to ensure that shared environments remain fundamentally human: contested, unpredictable, and alive.
